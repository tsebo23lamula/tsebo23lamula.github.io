01 — ABOUT

About

Biography

I am Tsebo Lamula, a geologist with interests in applied geophysics, hydrogeology, hydrogeochemistry, mine water and abandoned mine environments.

I hold a Postgraduate Diploma in Geology, an Advanced Diploma in Geology and a Diploma in Geology from the Tshwane University of Technology.

My academic and professional interests centre on the integration of geological, geophysical, hydrogeological and hydrogeochemical methods to investigate mining-affected environments.

Professional Experience

I have gained field and academic experience through geological, hydrogeological and hydrogeochemical investigations in mining and post-mining environments.

As a Research Assistant at Tshwane University of Technology, I participated in hydrogeological and hydrogeochemical investigations at the abandoned Havelock Asbestos Mine in eSwatini. My work included water sampling, tracer testing, tracer monitoring, dataset management and digitisation of historical mine records.

I also served as a Mineralogy Tutor at Tshwane University of Technology, providing practical and laboratory-based academic support to undergraduate geology students.

In the mining industry, I worked as a junior geologist in training at a gold mine in Randfontein, South Africa, where I undertook underground and open-pit geological duties under the supervision of the mine geologist.

03 — PROJECT

Edendale Lead Mine

Pretoria, South Africa

Postgraduate Diploma in Geology Research

My postgraduate research investigated the abandoned Edendale Lead Mine on the eastern outskirts of Pretoria, South Africa. The study focused on the characterisation of environmental and physical hazards associated with historical mining.

An integrated geophysical approach was used to investigate subsurface geological conditions, historical mining features and potential contamination pathways. The investigation incorporated magnetic, gravity, electrical resistivity tomography and seismic refraction tomography.

Hydrogeochemical investigations were undertaken to assess water quality within the study area. Geochemical modelling was conducted using PHREEQC, while hydrological tracer testing was used to quantify stream discharge and assess the contribution of mine water to the receiving stream.

05 — GAME

GAME

Geoscience of Abandoned Mine Environments

Research · Report · Inform · Advance

GAME (Geoscience of Abandoned Mine Environments) is an independent scientific publication dedicated to advancing knowledge of abandoned mine environments through interdisciplinary geoscience and evidence-based communication.

The publication examines geological, geophysical, hydrogeological and environmental processes associated with abandoned mines and their interactions with groundwater, surface water, soils and surrounding communities.
